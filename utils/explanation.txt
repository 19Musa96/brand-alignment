### Overview

This tool evaluates the relationship between two entities (people, companies, organizations, or brands) using semantic analysis of publicly available information.

We extract structured identity signals from Wikipedia and other sources and compare them across two dimensions:

• **Domain Relatedness**  
• **Value Alignment**

Each dimension is calculated by comparing multiple semantic axes using embeddings and weighted similarity scoring.

---

### Domain Relatedness

This measures whether two entities operate within the same ecosystem or market space.

The following axes contribute to the score:

**Primary Domain**  
The main industry or field in which the entity operates.  
Examples: football, automotive manufacturing, climate activism.

**Sub-domains**  
More specific operational areas within the broader domain.

**Economic Model**  
How the entity creates value or generates revenue.  
Examples: product sales, sponsorships, advertising, activism, services.

**Target Audience**  
The demographic groups the entity primarily engages with.

High domain relatedness typically indicates the entities participate in the same industry or cultural sphere.

---

### Value Alignment

This measures whether two entities share similar missions, beliefs, or cultural identities.

The following axes contribute to the score:

**Core Mission**  
The primary goals or objectives associated with the entity.

**Value Signals**  
Themes associated with the entity’s public messaging or reputation.

**Cultural Positioning**  
How the entity positions itself culturally.  
Examples: elite performance, grassroots activism, luxury, innovation.

**Power Positioning**  
Whether the entity represents institutional authority, disruption, advocacy, etc.

**Controversy Themes**  
Recurring public criticisms or controversies connected to the entity.

Value alignment reflects philosophical compatibility, not simply industry overlap.

---

### Scoring Approach

For each axis:

1. Text representations are converted into semantic embeddings
2. Similarity between entities is computed
3. Scores are weighted and aggregated across axes
4. Missing axes are ignored and weights are re-normalized

This produces two independent scores:

• **Domain Relatedness Score**  
• **Value Alignment Score**

These scores help identify potential brand partnerships, conflicts, or reputational risks.

---

### Interpretation

High relatedness + high alignment  
→ strong partnership potential

High relatedness + low alignment  
→ competitive or adversarial relationship

Low relatedness + high alignment  
→ shared values but different industries

Low relatedness + low alignment  
→ unrelated entities
